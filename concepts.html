Three directions

Same data, three personalities

Every concept does the same core job: log points, attach proof, discuss, rank. What changes is the main screen and how much ceremony an entry needs before it counts. Names and scores are example data.

01 · The Tribunal

Every entry is a charge. The group votes to uphold or throw it out. The groom is the judge.

02 · Matchday

The trip is a sports season. A live league table, daily MVP, and a forfeit zone.

03 · Receipts

A social feed of evidence posts. Reactions, threaded replies, and a three-tap quick add.
Concept 01

The Tribunal

Points are legal proceedings. Logging a penalty files a charge; logging a bonus files a commendation. Nothing hits the scoreboard until the jury (everyone except the accused) has had its say.

Core mechanicAn entry needs 2 votes to uphold, or it's thrown out after 3 objections. The groom gets one gavel per day to overrule any verdict.

Works because

  • Fewest arguments about fairness
  • The debate is part of the fun
  • Built-in role for the groom

Watch out

  • Slower: points wait for votes
  • Needs push notifications to work
Concept 02

Matchday

The trip is a short sports season. Each day is a matchday with its own table, and the overall standings roll up across the trip. Entries land instantly, like goals on a live ticker.

Core mechanicPoints count straight away. The bottom two each night sit in the forfeit zone and owe the group something the next morning (first round, breakfast run). Highest daily score is MVP.

Works because

  • Fast and instant
  • Daily resets keep stragglers in it
  • Stats are great for the end-of-trip recap

Watch out

  • No built-in way to dispute
  • Less room for photos on the main screen
Concept 03

Receipts

A private social feed where every post is evidence. The home screen is the photos and the banter; the leaderboard is a strip of faces pinned to the top. Logging is built around the camera: snap, tag, pick a rule, post.

Core mechanicPosts go up instantly as pending. They only hit the scoreboard once 3 other players tap Confirm. You can log points for anyone, including yourself. Reactions feed end-of-trip awards like "Most Laughed At".

Works because

  • Fastest to log in a loud bar
  • Doubles as the trip photo album
  • Comments and replies feel familiar

Watch out

  • Points wait for votes
  • Photo storage adds up
Chosen direction

Receipts, with the best bits borrowed

Logging is as fast as Receipts makes it, but nothing counts until the group backs it up. Ideas from the other two concepts fill in the rest.

  1. HomeThe Receipts feed with the faces strip, plus a Board tab that uses the Matchday league table.
  2. FairnessEvery entry, including ones you log for yourself, needs 3 confirmations from other players before it counts. Taken from the Tribunal.
  3. The groomPenalties count double and he gets one overrule per day.
  4. Daily rhythmMatchday's nightly forfeit zone and MVP, sent as a push notification at 06:00.
  5. The finaleEnd-of-trip awards and a recap page built from the stats and the most-reacted photos.

Scope

What goes in version one

MUST Before the trip

  • Join a trip with a 6-letter code, pick your name and face. No email signup.
  • Log points for a player by picking from the rulebook
  • Create a new rule while logging; it's added to the shared rulebook
  • Phone stays signed in after the first join
  • Attach photos and short videos as evidence
  • Comments with replies, plus emoji reactions
  • Live leaderboard that updates on every phone
  • Player history: every entry for and against them
  • Log points for yourself or anyone else
  • Confirm or reject votes: an entry counts at 3 confirms
  • "Needs your vote" list at the top of the feed
  • Push notification when someone logs against you or an entry needs your vote

NICE If there's time

  • Daily standings, forfeit zone and MVP
  • Offline queue: log with no signal, sends when back online
  • Admin tools for the organiser: edit rules, fix mistakes, undo
  • Auto badges for repeat offences
  • Daily challenges posted each morning

LATER After the trip

  • End-of-trip awards ceremony screen
  • Recap page with top photos and final table
  • Export all photos as one album
  • Reuse the app for the next trip or the wedding weekend
Build draft

How it gets built

AppReact Native with Expo and TypeScript. One codebase builds both the iPhone and Android app.
BackendSupabase: a Postgres database, photo and video storage, and realtime updates so the leaderboard moves live. The free tier covers a group this size.
Sign-inAnonymous sign-in plus a trip code and a 4-digit PIN. The phone stores a login token, so it stays signed in. More detail below.
PushExpo push notifications, sent from a database trigger when an entry is filed.
InstallAndroid: share an install file directly. iPhone: TestFlight, which needs an Apple Developer account ($99 a year).
Cheaper iPhone option: build it as an installable web app instead. Everyone adds it to their home screen from a link, it works on both platforms with no store and no $99 fee, and photo upload works. The trade-off is less reliable push notifications on iPhone.

How phones stay signed in

Each phone keeps a login token instead of a hardware ID. On first open, the app signs in anonymously and receives a token, stored in the phone's encrypted keychain (iOS) or keystore (Android). Every later open uses that token, so there's no login screen and no email.

First openEnter the trip code, tap your name, set a 4-digit PIN. The phone is now linked to your player.
New or wiped phoneEnter the trip code, tap your name, type your PIN. The new phone gets linked too. Forgot the PIN? The organiser resets it.
Why not a device IDPhones don't hand apps a permanent hardware ID. The IDs they do give reset when the app is reinstalled, and Apple restricts tracking with them. The token plus PIN does the job without that.

To decide

Questions for the group

  1. How many people? With 5 players, only 3 can vote on most entries, so all of them would have to confirm.
  2. Do pending entries stay open forever, or expire (e.g. rejected if not confirmed within 24 hours)?
  3. Does the groom's daily overrule skip the 3-vote check?
  4. Is everyone on iPhone, Android, or a mix?
  5. Is anyone happy to pay $99 for the Apple account, or go the web-app route?
  6. Does the groom get a special role (judge, double penalties, both)?
  7. What happens to the last-place finisher? The app can display the forfeit.
  8. Trip dates? That sets the deadline for the test run.
  9. Should the photos be private to the group forever, or deleted after a set time?
